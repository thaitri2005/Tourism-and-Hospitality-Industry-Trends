# Methodology (summary paragraph)

Data were collected from the nine tourism and hospitality websites specified in the brief (UN Tourism, WTTC, IH&RA, PATA, AHLA, the European Travel Commission, the Caribbean Tourism Organization, Hospitality Net and Travel Weekly), covering English-language news articles, press releases and blog posts published between 10 October 2016 and 10 October 2026. A purpose-built Python crawler listed article pages from each site's sitemaps and paginated archives and downloaded them at a polite rate (at least three seconds between requests). Where a site blocks automated access (CTO, Travel Weekly) or its older archives are no longer online (WTTC, AHLA and PATA before 2019-2020), archived copies were retrieved from the Internet Archive's Wayback Machine (2,692 articles); high-volume Hospitality Net and Travel Weekly were sampled at random across the decade. Headlines, dates and main text were extracted, and only dated English articles of at least 120 words were kept; duplicates and near-duplicates (TF-IDF cosine similarity of 0.94 or more) were removed, leaving 9,060 articles from 8 websites (IH&RA published no original news in the period). Themes were coded with a transparent dictionary of 19 industry themes; an article counted as discussing a theme when a theme term appeared in its headline, twice in its opening 80 words, or at least three times using two different terms. Theme prominence was measured as the share of articles discussing each theme, calculated within each website and averaged with equal weight per website, for the last twelve months (current themes), for each year from 2016 to 2026 (trends) and for each website (community focus). Sentiment towards each theme and brand was estimated with a RoBERTa transformer sentiment model applied to the sentences that mention it. Brands were identified with a dictionary built from the Global Brands Magazine 2026 list of the top ten tourism brands and their sub-brands, extended to other hotel, airline, cruise and travel-technology companies; brand associations were measured from the themes and words appearing within one sentence of each brand mention. An unsupervised topic model (TF-IDF with non-negative matrix factorisation) was used as a cross-check on the dictionary, and a stratified random validation sample of 64 articles and 120 sentences, coded independently, gave a theme-coding precision of 75%, a recall of 71% and 85% agreement on sentiment.
